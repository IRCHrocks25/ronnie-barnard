Proof

Results

What looked wrong. What was actually wrong.

Three initiatives. Three problems hiding somewhere else.

Case 01 Financial services

Customer onboarding cut from 31 days to under 7.

What it looked like
Buggy software. The platform had started life as a prototype, and three developers were patching it directly in production. Every new customer brought more defects, manual work, and support calls.
What it really was
A platform and a team never designed for scale. Growth was creating work faster than the business could absorb it.
What changed
We stabilized the existing platform and, in parallel, built a replacement designed around each customer’s path through the business. The technology team grew from 3 people to more than 50 in about a year.
The result
Onboarding fell from about 31 days to under 7, with straightforward cases processed in about 30 minutes. Monthly revenue grew to roughly 16 times its starting level. The technology didn’t create that growth alone. It removed the ceiling on it.
Case 02 Compliance technology

From one new customer a month to many at once.

What it looked like
A technology team working against the owner. The team said it could onboard only one new customer a month.
What it really was
Each new customer meant rebuilding thousands of configuration settings by hand, even though customers in the same industry shared most of them.
What changed
We created templates for each industry from existing clients and adjusted them. Within about a month, the onboarding ceiling was gone.
The result
Customers could be onboarded in parallel. The company opened a U.S. operation, built a platform serving more than 10,000 users, and grew annual revenue from about $1.2 million to $3.8 million over four years.
Case 03 State government

A day and a half of analysis, answered in 20 seconds.

What it looked like
Slow reporting. The plan on the table was a better data warehouse.
What it really was
The delay between a leader asking a new question and getting an answer. Every new question still waited in a developer’s queue.
What changed
We built a secure AI layer over more than 5 billion records across about 20,000 fields. The agency’s report writers turned their knowledge of the data into definitions the AI could use.
The result
Authorized staff can ask questions by text or voice and get answers in real time. One analysis that took an experienced employee about a day and a half was reproduced by the system in about 20 seconds.
Sometimes the answer comes fast.

A founding team hit a wall before launch. Their AI product kept giving confused, made-up answers, and nothing they tried fixed it. Thirty minutes into our first call, the cause was clear: the AI’s core instructions were being rewritten in the middle of each conversation. They launched a few weeks later.

How I work

Five steps from uncertainty to measurable value.

The Technology Unlock Framework is a disciplined sequence for initiatives that are underperforming, overcomplicated, or not delivering the value expected. It starts with the business outcome you’re trying to reach, not with a technology someone has already chosen.

Step 01

Decode

What is really happening?

I build a clear picture of the outcome you want, the system you have, and every fix already tried.

Step 02

Isolate

What is actually holding it back?

Most struggling initiatives show dozens of visible problems. Usually only one or two are limiting everything else.

Step 03

Reframe

What problem should we be solving?

We redefine the problem around its real cause. That may confirm the current plan, change it, or stop it.

Step 04

Unlock

What must change now?

We identify and guide the change with the greatest leverage — architecture, workflow, team structure, automation, AI, or a single decision.

Step 05

Realize

What can the business do now that it couldn’t before?

Success is measured in business outcomes: launches, productivity, capacity, risk reduction, growth, and investment value. Not in software shipped.

Experience spots the pattern fast. Evidence earns the fix.

The engagements

Ways to work together

Start with a clear diagnosis. Go as far as you need.

Every engagement is led by me personally, delivered mostly remotely, and built around one question: what is actually preventing this from succeeding?

01

Technology Initiative Diagnostic

For: Leaders who need to know why an important initiative isn’t delivering as expected.

What happens: Executive and team interviews, a review of goals and progress to date, an assessment of past fixes, and a look at architecture and team structure where relevant.

You leave with: A clear, evidence-based answer to what’s actually stopping progress, presented in an executive findings session and documented with key risks.

Format: Defined scope, not open-ended. Delivered remotely.

Recommended starting point
02

Technology Initiative Diagnostic + Direction

For: Leaders who know they need to act and want to act on the right thing.

What happens: Everything in the Diagnostic, plus a reframed problem statement, options, a recommended direction, priorities, decision points, sequencing, ownership, and initial implementation guidance.

You leave with: What’s really wrong, what should change, and what happens next.

03

Technology Initiative Guided Unlock

For: Organizations that want me alongside while the fix is carried out.

What happens: Executive advisory sessions, architecture guidance, progress reviews, course correction, knowledge transfer, and team training. AI guidance where AI is the right answer.

You leave with: Measurable progress, tracked against the business outcome agreed at the start.

Format: Mostly remote. From a short intervention to several months, generally no more than six.

04

Executive Technology Advisory

For: CEOs, boards, and founders facing consequential technology decisions.

Typical questions: Continue, redirect, or stop? Stabilize, rebuild, or replace? Is the roadmap credible? Is AI the right move here?

You leave with: Experienced, independent judgment before you commit, including a Reframe Document: the new problem definition, the future architecture that removes the constraint, and the roadmap to get there.

05

Investment & Portfolio Technology Advisory

For: PE and VC firms, operating partners, and advisory teams.

Pre-investment: Technical and product diligence covering scalability, architecture, team, technology risk, and the assumptions behind the investment.

Post-investment: Portfolio technology assessment, constraint identification, technology value creation, and intervention when key initiatives stall.

You leave with: A clear view of whether the technology can carry the investment thesis, and what it will take to get there.

I work hands-on with your leaders and technology teams. I don’t supply developers or outsourced build capacity.

About Ronnie

What you see depends on where you stand.

I grew up on a farm in South Africa, far from city lights. Every night, the Milky Way filled the sky. The first time I looked up from a city, I could count the stars on two hands. The others hadn’t gone anywhere. The noise was hiding them.

Technology initiatives work the same way. The cause is usually there. It’s just buried under the noise.

I wrote my first business system at nine: bookkeeping and point of sale for my mother’s shop, saved on cassette tape. She ran her shop on it for a long time.

Since then, I’ve worked as a developer, architect, CTO, CIO, and advisor across four continents. I was employee number nine at a medical device company that grew from about $300,000 to $80 million in annual revenue in under five years, and I helped launch three versions of its product. I’ve designed a world-first electronic vehicle identification system and high-volume financial platforms, and architected AI that turned a state agency’s data into answers on demand.

At every level, I saw the same pattern: capable people working hard on the wrong problem, getting better at working around it instead of removing it. That frustration became my work.

Today I work from Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas, with clients in the U.S. and beyond. I take on a small number of engagements each year, so every client gets my full attention.
